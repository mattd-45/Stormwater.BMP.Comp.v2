## All 14 Jurisdictions

Sales reference document — confirm all details against current published guidance before use in submittals.

# 1. New York City, NY

## Retention

**Regulatory Framework:** NYC Unified Stormwater Rule (USWR), effective February 2022.

**Triggers:**
- Disturb ≥20,000 sq ft of soil, OR
- Create ≥5,000 sq ft of new impervious surface, OR
- Subject to Local Law 92/94 (Sustainable Roofing Zone)

**Water Quality Volume (WQv):**
- Retain runoff from a 1.5-inch rainfall event (MS4 areas)
- Sewer Operations Volume (CSS areas): manage runoff from a 1.85-inch storm event

**Compliance Hierarchy:**
1. Infiltration (requires soil rate >0.5 in/hr)
2. Evapotranspiration
3. Reuse/Recycling
4. Treatment/Filtration

**Green Roof Credits:**
- Classified as Tier 1 Stormwater Management Practices (SMPs) for both CSS and MS4 projects
- Reduce stormwater runoff by 50–90%
- DEP Green Infrastructure Grant Program reimburses up to 100% of installation costs for eligible projects (minimum 3,500 SF, minimum 1.5" soil depth)

**Purple-Roof Modeling Values (DEP submittals):**
- Engineered Soil Media: 0.20 cf/cf available porosity
- Needled Mineral Wool (NMW): 0.40 cf/cf (previously approved DEP value)
- Honeycomb Reservoir: 0.93 cf/cf (detention credit)
- Drain Box Orifice (refined): 0.07 (Purple-Roof drain gates)

## Detention

**Design Storm:** Historically 2-year storm control; modern designs reference 5-year and 10-year storms.

**Release Rates (CSS areas):**
- Maximum release: greater of 0.25 cfs OR 10% of Allowable Flow
- If Allowable Flow <0.25 cfs, maximum release = Allowable Flow
- Peak flow release rates are formula-based; weighted coefficient as low as 0.10

**MS4 Areas:**
- Site stormwater flow rate restricted to Allowable Flow

**Context:** NYC's combined sewer system covers approximately 60% of the city. USWR aims to reduce peak flows and prevent Combined Sewer Overflows (CSOs).

## PV (Bare Roof / Vegetated Roof)

**Local Laws 92/94 (2019, Climate Mobilization Act):**
- 100% of the Sustainable Roofing Zone must be covered with solar PV, green roof, or combination
- Applies to all new buildings and major roof replacements
- Minimum solar capacity: 4 kW to qualify as compliant solar alternative (below 4 kW must use green roof)
- Maximum roof slope: 2:12
- Minimum continuous roof area trigger: 200 SF (100 SF for Group R buildings ≤5 stories)

**Solar Property Tax Abatement:**
- 7.5% per year for 4 consecutive years
- Maximum annual abatement: $62,500 or annual property taxes (whichever is lower)
- Total cap: up to 30% of eligible costs, maximum $250,000
- Eligible: Tax Classes 1, 2, and 4

**NY-Sun Incentives (NYSERDA):**
- Con Edison territory (NYC): ~$0.30/watt residential, ~$0.40/watt commercial
- Rates decrease as megawatt blocks fill; check NYSERDA dashboard for current rates

**Net Metering:**
- 1:1 retail rate credit for excess solar energy; monthly credits roll over 12 months
- Systems installed before VDER residential rollout locked in net metering for 20 years
- Large commercial/industrial (>10 kW): mandatory Value of Distributed Energy Resources (VDER) Value Stack Tariff

**Bio-Solar:**
- Integration of solar panels with green roofs meets LL 92/94 dual requirement
- Green roofs cool solar panels, improving PV efficiency
- Combined system achieves both energy generation and runoff reduction

**Key Links:**
- [NYC DEP Unified Stormwater Rule](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dep/water/unified-stormwater-rule.page)
- [NYC Buildings - LL 92/94](https://www.nyc.gov/site/buildings/codes/ll92-solar-green-roofs.page)
- [NYSERDA NY-Sun Program](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/All-Programs/NY-Sun)

# 2. Washington, DC

## Retention

**Regulatory Framework:** DOEE Stormwater Management Regulations and Guidebook.

**Stormwater Retention Volume (SWRv):**
- New development: retain 1.2 inches
- Major substantial improvements/renovations: retain 0.8 inches
- Applies to sites disturbing ≥5,000 sq ft

**Compliance Pathways:**
- Up to 50% of retention can be achieved off-site (with DOEE approval)
- Payments-in-lieu of off-site retention available
- Stormwater Retention Credit (SRC) trading program

**Stormwater Retention Credits (SRCs):**
- 1 SRC = 1 gallon of GI retention capacity for 1 year
- Market-based, tradable units certified by DOEE
- CSS sites: can achieve 100% of retention with SRCs from MS4 area
- MS4 sites: minimum 50% on-site before using SRCs
- Green roofs on voluntary projects in MS4 area generate High-Impact SRCs (greater value)

**Green Roof Incentives:**
- RiverSmart Rewards: DC Water bill discounts for voluntary green roofs
- Rebates: $15/sq ft in Wards 7, 8, and MS4 area (no size cap)

## Detention

**Peak Flow Control:**
- 2-year, 24-hour storm: post-development peak ≤ pre-development peak (channel protection)
- 15-year, 24-hour storm: design capacity of District's sewer conveyance system (flood protection)

**System Context:**
- Combined Sewer System (CSS): serves ~1/3 of District
- Municipal Separate Storm Sewer System (MS4): serves ~2/3 of District
- Current MS4 NPDES permit effective December 20, 2023; expires December 19, 2028

## PV (Bare Roof / Vegetated Roof)

**Clean Energy DC Omnibus Act (2018):**
- Goal: 100% of District's energy from Tier 1 renewable sources by 2032
- 2026 targets: minimum 59% Tier 1 renewable, minimum 5% solar

**Local Solar Expansion Amendment Act of 2022:**
- Increases solar carve-out from 10% to 15% by 2041

**Solar Renewable Energy Credits (SRECs):**
- 1 SREC per MWh of solar electricity generated
- Market value: up to ~$400/SREC
- Significant revenue stream for building owners with solar

**Net Metering:**
- Full retail rate credit for excess energy; 12-month credit banking
- Administered by PEPCO

**Green Area Ratio (GAR):**
- Score-based requirement for new buildings
- Score requirements range from 0.2 to 0.4 depending on zone
- Green roofs count toward GAR score with specific multiplier values

**Building Energy Performance Standards (BEPS):**
- Applies to all buildings >10,000 sq ft
- Sets minimum energy performance thresholds
- Three consecutive 6-year BEPS Periods

**Solar for All Program:**
- Free solar PV installation for households at or below 80% AMI
- ~50% electricity bill reduction over 15-year period

**Key Links:**
- [DOEE Stormwater Guidebook](https://doee.dc.gov/swguidebook)
- [DOEE SRC Trading Program](https://doee.dc.gov/src)
- [DOEE Solar in the District](https://doee.dc.gov/service/solar-district)

# 3. Chicago, IL

## Retention

**Regulatory Framework:** 2024 DWM Stormwater Regulations (Chicago Municipal Code Chapter 11-18).

**Triggers:**
- Land disturbance ≥15,000 sq ft in aggregate, OR
- New contiguous impervious area ≥7,500 sq ft, OR
- Any discharge of stormwater into waters or separate sewer system

**Volume Control:**
- Capture the first 0.5 inches of runoff from all impervious surfaces with approved BMPs
- Alternative path: provide ≥15% reduction in total impervious area vs. existing conditions
- Green roofs explicitly recognized as approved Volume Control BMPs

## Detention

**Design Storm:** 100-year storm event.

**Release Rate Caps:**
- Sites ≤0.5 acres: max 0.15 cfs (standard 3-inch vortex restrictor)
- Sites 0.5–1.75 acres: interpolated between 0.15–0.25 cfs (4-inch vortex restrictor)
- At-grade open space: 1.0 cfs/acre

**Lot-to-Lot Buildings:**
- Where structures occupy >85% of the lot, rooftop storage may be based on the 10-year storm, provided 100-year overflows are safely routed

**Vortex Restrictors:**
- 3-inch opening: 0.15 cfs max
- 4-inch opening: 0.25 cfs max
- Must fit into 8-inch diameter receiving pipe
- Minimum plate orifice: 2.5 inches diameter

**Exemptions:** Sites discharging to Lake Michigan, Lake Calumet, or the Calumet River north of O'Brien Lock and Dam have no rate control requirements.

## PV (Bare Roof / Vegetated Roof)

**Green Roof / FAR Bonuses:**
- Green roofs covering >50% of net roof area (or minimum 2,000 SF contiguous) qualify for FAR bonus of up to 2 FAR in downtown mixed-use districts
- Zoning Code 17-4-1015

**Green Permit Program:**
- Expedited permitting (fewer than 30 business days vs. 60+)
- Possible permit fee reduction
- Possible waiver of consultant code review fees up to $25,000

**Solar-Ready Building Code (effective January 1, 2023):**
- Applies to flat/low-sloped roofs (<2:12) on new buildings with footprint ≥7,500 SF and height ≤60 feet
- Solar-ready zone: at least 40% of available roof area
- Structural capacity: minimum 5 psf dead load
- Electrical infrastructure: conduit from roof to main panel, reserved breaker space labeled "For Future Solar Electric and Storage"
- Exempt if available roof area <2,000 SF

**Illinois Shines / Adjustable Block Program:**
- SREC payments over 15 years of operation
- Chicago area (ComEd territory): up to ~$10,000 in SREC payments for residential
- Residential/small commercial: ~$300/kW installed capacity

**ComEd Net Metering:**
- Credit for excess electricity at applicable rates; month-to-month carryover
- Transitioning from full retail rate in 2025; existing customers locked in
- Smart Inverter Rebate: $300/kW for new installations

**Property Tax:**
- Illinois residential solar: property tax exemption with no dollar limit (added home value entirely exempt)
- C-PACE financing available for commercial properties

**Key Links:**
- [Chicago 2024 Stormwater Manual (PDF)](https://www.chicago.gov/content/dam/city/depts/water/general/Engineering/SewerConstStormReq/2024StormwaterManual.pdf)
- [Chicago 2024 Stormwater Regulations (PDF)](https://www.chicago.gov/content/dam/city/depts/water/general/Engineering/SewerConstStormReq/2024Regulations.pdf)
- [Illinois Shines Program](https://illinoisshines.com/)

# 4. Boston, MA

## Retention

**Regulatory Framework:** BWSC (Boston Water and Sewer Commission) stormwater requirements; MassDEP Stormwater Management Standards.

**Triggers:**
- Properties with >400 sq ft impervious surface: stormwater charge triggered
- >2,500 sq ft impervious surface: drainage analysis required
- Article 80 Large Project Review (>50,000 sq ft GFA): Article 37 green building applies
- Article 80 Small Project Review (20,000–50,000 sq ft or ≥15 residential units)

**Retention Requirements:**
- Projects <100,000 sq ft floor area: retain runoff from 1.0 inch of rainfall × total impervious area
- Projects ≥100,000 sq ft floor area: retain runoff from 1.25 inches of rainfall × total impervious area

**MassDEP Standards:**
- 80% removal of average annual post-construction Total Suspended Solids (TSS)
- Minimum soil infiltration rate: 0.17 in/hr at actual infiltration location

**Green Roof Credits:**
- Up to 30% credit on stormwater fees for demonstrated BMP use including green roofs
- Green roofs reduce peak flow rates by 50–90% vs. conventional roofs

## Detention

**Peak Flow Control:**
- Post-development peak discharge ≤ pre-development rate for 2-year and 10-year, 24-hour storms
- 10-year design storm = 5.15 inches of rainfall
- Evaluation also required for 100-year event per Massachusetts Wetland Protection Act

**CSS Requirements:**
- 155 miles of combined sewer in Boston system
- New connections with design flows ≥15,000 gpd must remove I/I at 4:1 ratio (4 gallons removed per 1 gallon new wastewater)

## PV (Bare Roof / Vegetated Roof)

**Article 37 Green Building Requirements:**
- Projects ≥20,000 sq ft GFA or ≥15 dwelling units must be LEED "certifiable"

**Net Zero Carbon (NZC) Zoning (projects filed on/after July 1, 2025):**
- ≥50% of flat or south-facing roof area must have solar energy system
- 90% of uncovered parking structure decks must have solar coverage
- Projects ≥50,000 sq ft must conduct embodied carbon life cycle assessment

**BERDO (Building Emissions Reduction and Disclosure Ordinance):**
- Non-residential buildings ≥20,000 sq ft; residential ≥15 units
- Compliance starts 2025 or 2030 depending on building class
- Net-zero emissions target by 2050
- On-site solar is "most effective strategy" per BERDO guidance

**SMART Program (Solar Massachusetts Renewable Target) 3.0:**
- Fixed, performance-based rate per kWh for 10 years (residential) or 20 years (commercial)
- 2026 Program Year: 900 MW available capacity
- Adders: battery storage ~$0.04/kWh, building-mounted $0.02/kWh, low-income double rate
- Eligible: Eversource, National Grid, Unitil customers

**Property Tax & Incentives:**
- 30% federal ITC
- 15% Massachusetts state income tax credit (up to $1,000)
- Solar systems exempt from property taxes for 20 years
- Sales tax exemption on solar equipment
- Mass Save ConnectedSolutions: $275/kW battery compensation (June–September peak)

**Key Links:**
- [BWSC Storm Drain Requirements](https://www.bwsc.org/builders-contractors/site-plan-requirements/storm-drain-requirements)
- [Article 37 Green Building](https://www.bostonplans.org/planning-zoning/planning-initiatives/article-37-green-building-guidelines)
- [SMART Program](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/solar-massachusetts-renewable-target-smart)

# 5. Philadelphia, PA

## Retention

**Regulatory Framework:** PWD Stormwater Management Guidance Manual (Version 3.1).

**Triggers:**
- ≥15,000 sq ft of earth disturbance (citywide), OR
- ≥5,000 sq ft in Darby/Cobbs Creeks or Wissahickon Creek watersheds

**Water Quality Volume:**
- Retain the first 1.5 inches of runoff from all Directly Connected Impervious Areas (DCIA)
- If infiltration feasible: 100% of WQv must be infiltrated
- If infiltration infeasible (CSS areas): 100% of non-infiltrated WQv must be treated; maximum release rate of 0.05 cfs per acre of DCIA

**Channel Protection:** Control peak flow rates for the 1-year, 24-hour storm.

**Flood Control:** Peak rate control for 10-year and 100-year storms. Positive overflow required for 100-year event.

**20% Rule (Redevelopment):** Projects reducing impervious area by ≥20% (pre-development vs. post-development DCIA) are exempt from Flood Control and Channel Protection requirements.

**Green Roof Design Credits:**
- Minimum 3 inches of engineered growing media
- Runoff coefficients: 0.10–0.30 (vs. 0.95 for conventional impervious roof)
- Classified as "Highest Preference" stormwater management practice
- Can disconnect other rooftop areas from sewer system

## Detention

**Channel Protection:** 1-year, 24-hour storm peak flow control.

**Flood Control:** 10-year and 100-year storm peak flow control. Post-development peaks must not exceed pre-development rates.

**Release Rate:** Maximum 0.05 cfs per acre of DCIA when routing 1.7-inch PWD Design Storm (for non-infiltrating areas).

**Act 167:** Watershed-specific peak rate requirements per Pennsylvania Stormwater Management Act Flood Management Districts.

## PV (Bare Roof / Vegetated Roof)

**No citywide solar mandate.** C-PACE and local programs favor projects combining solar PV with green roofs.

**Green Roof Tax Credit:**
- 50% of all construction costs for green roof
- Up to $100,000 per project
- Applied against Business Income and Receipts Tax (BIRT)
- Green roof must cover ≥60% of building's rooftop

**Expedited Green Review (5-Day):**
- Available for projects with ≥95% green infrastructure
- Surface Green Review: 5-day review during PCSMP phase with option to delay infiltration testing
- Disconnection Green Review: 5-day review with infiltration testing exemption

**Zoning & Density Bonuses:**
- Up to 25% increase in allowable residential dwelling units for green roofs covering ≥60% of rooftop
- Eligible in RM-2, RM-3, RM-4 and mixed-use/multifamily zones
- Parking landscaping credits: interior parking lot landscaping reduced sq-ft-for-sq-ft by green roof area

**Pennsylvania SRECs:**
- 1 SREC per 1 MWh of solar electricity produced
- Current pricing: ~$31.25/SREC (market range $25–$35)
- 3-year validity period

**Net Metering (PECO):**
- Full retail rate credit for excess kWh exports
- Monthly credit rollover; annual true-up May 31
- No cap on excess power purchased by utility
- Hardware credit: up to $400 for second meter installation

**C-PACE Financing:**
- Covers 100% of hard and soft costs for solar/energy projects
- Repayment over up to 30 years on property tax bill
- Available for commercial, multifamily, retail, warehouse, medical, hospitality, industrial, and nonprofit properties
- Enabled by Philadelphia City Council legislation (June 2019)

**Philadelphia Solar Rebate Program:**
- Currently inactive (no active funding); applications accepted for waitlist
- Residential: $0.20/watt; Commercial: $0.10/watt; Max $100,000/project

**Property Tax Note:** Pennsylvania does NOT provide property tax exemptions for solar PV systems. Solar installations may increase assessed property value.

**Key Links:**
- [PWD Stormwater Management Manual](https://water.phila.gov/development/stormwater-plan-review/manual/)
- [Green Roof Tax Credit](https://www.phila.gov/services/payments-assistance-taxes/taxes/tax-credits/business-tax-credits/green-roof-tax-credit/)
- [Philadelphia Energy Authority](https://philaenergy.org/)

# 6. Nashville, TN

## Retention

**Regulatory Framework:** Metro Water Services (MWS) Stormwater Management Manual (5 volumes, updated 2021); Nashville Metropolitan Code Chapter 15.64.

**Triggers:**
- Projects disturbing >10,000 sq ft required to design, install, and maintain stormwater quality and quantity controls
- Infill development: 800–15,000 sq ft of additional net impervious area for residential purposes

**Runoff Reduction Volume (RRv):**
- Capture and manage the first 1.1 inches of rainfall using LID practices to the "maximum extent practicable"
- Projects meeting 80% runoff reduction goal through LID are presumed compliant

**Water Quality:**
- Structural SCMs must achieve 80% Total Suspended Solids (TSS) removal
- Pretreatment approval level: 50% TSS removal

**LID Waiver:**
- Required when site constraints prevent full 1.1" RRv compliance
- Submit electronically to MWS; alternative compliance requires engineered 80% TSS removal

**Green Roof as SCM:**
- Recognized as structural Stormwater Control Measure
- Typical green roofs capture approximately 55% of annual rainfall

## Detention

**Peak Flow Control:**
- Post-development peaks must not exceed pre-development rates for the 2, 10, 25, 50, and 100-year, 24-hour storms
- Pre- and post-development hydrographs required for all design storms
- Downstream impact analysis required at the point where the site makes up ≤10% of total drainage area

**Release Rates:** Project-specific based on pre-development hydrology (no uniform prescriptive rate). Outlet control structures (orifice plates, weirs, outlet pipes) establish maximum allowable discharge for each design storm.

## PV (Bare Roof / Vegetated Roof)

**No municipal solar mandate.**

**Green Roof Stormwater Rebate:**
- $10 per sq ft of installed vegetative roof
- Credit applied to monthly sewer charges
- Credit duration: 60 months or until maximum total reached, whichever is first
- Example: 5,000 SF green roof = up to $50,000 total credit

**Stormwater User Fee Credit:**
- Up to 50% reduction of the stormwater user fee (Ordinance BL2009-407)
- Eligible practices: green roofs, permeable pavement, bioretention, tree preservation, infiltration, rainwater harvesting

**TVA Programs:**
- Green Invest: utility-scale procurement, up to 100% of energy load, 20-year REC contracts
- Green Switch: match 100% of electricity use with solar for $2/month premium
- Dispersed Power Production (DPP): TVA purchases excess residential solar at ~2¢/kWh (avoided cost, adjusted monthly — well below retail)

**Net Metering:** Tennessee does NOT offer traditional net metering and has no standardized interconnection rules at the state level. TVA-served customers receive compensation through DPP program only.

**Property Tax:** Solar system added value assessed at only 12.5% of actual installation cost. Automatic — no action required. Cannot be combined with battery storage.

**Sales Tax:** Solar panel systems exempt from all sales tax in Tennessee.

**SolSmart:** Nashville designated SolSmart Bronze (2022) for streamlined solar permitting. Permits start at $75.

**Community Solar:** Music City Solar (NES) — 2 MW capacity, subscription at $166.63/panel one-time cost, monthly energy credit on bill.

**Key Links:**
- [MWS Stormwater Management Manual](https://www.nashville.gov/departments/water/developers/stormwater-review/stormwater-management-manual)
- [Solar Panel Permits](https://www.nashville.gov/departments/codes/construction-and-permits/trades-permits/solar-panel-photovoltaic-permits)
- [TVA Valley Renewable Energy](https://www.tva.com/energy/valley-renewable-energy)

# 7. Seattle, WA

## Retention

**Regulatory Framework:** Seattle Stormwater Code (SMC 22.800–22.808); 2021 Seattle Stormwater Manual (Directors' Rule 10-2021).

**Triggers:**
- ≥2,000 sq ft new/replaced impervious surface: GSI required to MEF; 25-year event management (lake basins)
- ≥5,000 sq ft: drainage report by licensed civil engineer required
- ≥9,000 sq ft developmental coverage: large project requirements
- ≥10,000 sq ft: continuous runoff modeling required (Seattle 158-year precipitation/evaporation series)

**Green Stormwater Infrastructure (GSI) to Maximum Extent Feasible (MEF):**
- MEF = fully implemented, constrained only by physical site limitations, practical engineering design, and reasonable financial/environmental considerations
- Required for all projects with ≥2,000 SF new/replaced impervious surface

**Pre-Development/Pasture Hydrology Standard:**
- Post-developed flows must be ≤ pre-developed pasture flows within the 1–10% exceedance flow range
- Calculation using approved models (WWHM 2012, MGS Flood) with pass/fail result

**Green Roof Credits:**
- Green Factor multiplier: 0.7 for rooftop plantings at least one floor above grade
- Volume calculated from green roof area × growing medium depth/porosity × drainage layer depth/porosity

## Detention

**Peak Flow Control:**
- 1–10% exceedance standard: post-developed discharge rates must not exceed pre-developed pasture discharge rates across this flow duration range
- Flow duration matching required between pre- and post-developed conditions

**Detention Facilities:**
- Cannot serve as sole stormwater management solution — must be combined with operational stormwater management (OSM) practices
- Must demonstrate OSM practices are infeasible or insufficient before relying solely on detention

## PV (Bare Roof / Vegetated Roof)

**Solar-Ready Building Code:**
- Commercial buildings ≤20 stories: minimum 40% of net roof area designated as solar zone
- Alternative: smaller of 40% of roof area OR 20% of electrical service size
- System sizing: 0.50 watts/sq ft of conditioned space
- Structural: 4 psf additional dead load within solar zone
- Residential: Appendix T of 2021 Seattle Residential Code covers detached and townhouse solar-ready provisions

**Seattle Green Factor:**
- Score-based landscaping code requirement
- Minimum scores by zone: 0.30 (commercial), 0.50 (midrise/highrise residential), 0.60 (lowrise residential)
- Green roofs: 0.7 multiplier

**Living Building Pilot:**
- Voluntary program (expires December 31, 2030 or 20 projects enrolled)
- Height bonuses: +12.5 ft residential / +15 ft non-residential (zones ≤85 ft); +25 ft / +30 ft (zones >85 ft)

**Washington State Solar Incentives:**
- Sales tax exemption: 100% for systems <100 kW (through December 2029); 50% for 100–500 kW
- Property tax exemption: only for facilities ≥10 MW nameplate capacity (10 or 15-year term)

**Seattle City Light Net Metering:**
- Bill credits for solar energy sent to grid; systems <100 kW
- Per RCW 80.60.030

**HOA Solar Rights (RCW 64.38.055):** HOA prohibitions on solar panels are void and unenforceable. HOAs may only restrict panels extending above roofline or not matching roof slope.

**Community Solar:** Seattle City Light program in design phase. Washington Community Solar Expansion Program: $100 million for development, minimum 10-year benefit period.

**Key Links:**
- [Seattle Stormwater Code - SDCI](https://www.seattle.gov/sdci/codes/codes-we-enforce-(a-z)/stormwater-code)
- [Seattle Energy Code](https://www.seattle.gov/sdci/codes/codes-we-enforce-(a-z)/energy-code)
- [Seattle Green Factor](https://www.seattle.gov/sdci/codes/codes-we-enforce-(a-z)/seattle-green-factor)

# 8. San Francisco, CA

## Retention

**Regulatory Framework:** SF Stormwater Management Ordinance (Public Works Code, Article 4.2, Sections 147–147.6); Stormwater Management Requirements and Design Guidelines (SMR).

**Triggers:**
- Separate sewer areas: ≥2,500 sq ft of new/replaced impervious surface (Small: 2,500–5,000; Large: ≥5,000)
- Combined sewer areas: ≥5,000 sq ft (Large Projects only)
- All Large Projects require Stormwater Control Plan (SCP) submitted to SFPUC

**Separate Sewer Areas:**
- Design storm: capture and treat runoff from a 0.75-inch, 24-hour rainfall event

**Combined Sewer Areas:**
- 25% reduction in peak flow rate and volume from pre-development conditions for the 2-year, 24-hour design storm
- Applies to sites with existing imperviousness >50%

**Green Roof Design:**
- Minimum growing media depth: 4 inches
- Vegetation: high species diversity, native species, low water use
- Full square footage counts toward stormwater compliance

## Detention

**Sites with ≤50% Existing Imperviousness:**
- Post-development peak flow and volume must not exceed pre-development conditions for 1-year and 2-year, 24-hour storms

**Sites with >50% Existing Imperviousness:**
- Post-development peak flow and volume decreased by 25% from pre-development conditions for 2-year, 24-hour storm

**Release Rates:** Project-specific through hydrologic calculations in Stormwater Control Plan; must align with receiving sewer system capacity.

## PV (Bare Roof / Vegetated Roof)

**Better Roofs Ordinance (Ordinance 221-16, effective January 1, 2017):**
- First U.S. citywide solar/living roof mandate
- Option 1: 15% of roof area as Solar PV, OR
- Option 2: 30% of roof area as Living Roof (green roof), OR
- Option 3: Combination (1 sq ft living roof = 0.5 sq ft toward solar equivalent)
- Applies to: non-residential ≥2,000 sq ft and residential of any size, both with ≤10 occupied floors

**California Title 24 (Statewide):**
- Solar PV required on most new residential and commercial buildings
- Solar-ready area: minimum 15% of total roof area
- Battery storage required on new buildings with PV (effective January 1, 2023)

**GoSolarSF Program (SFPUC):**
- Nearly $30 million distributed; 6,000 systems installed
- Currently active primarily for low-income DAC-SASH (no-cost installation)
- EJ Bonus: $2,000 additional in EJ zip codes 94107 and 94124
- Inverter replacement rebates: up to $3,000 for eligible low-income customers

**Net Energy Metering 3.0 (NEM 3.0, effective mid-April 2023):**
- 75–80% less compensation for exported solar vs. NEM 2.0
- Export rate: ~$0.05/kWh (vs. ~$0.30/kWh under NEM 2.0)
- Payback period extended from 5–7 years to 8–12 years
- Battery storage increasingly important for ROI

**SFPUC Green Infrastructure Grants:**
- Up to $2.5 million per project / $2 million per applicant
- Covers design and construction of green infrastructure including green roofs
- Post-installation stormwater credits reduce monthly sewer bills

**Property Tax:** Active solar systems NOT assessed as property improvements — no tax increase. Must install by January 1, 2027 to qualify.

**PACE Financing:** 10–30 year terms; repaid through property tax bill; transfers with property sale.

**Key Links:**
- [SFPUC Stormwater Management](https://www.sfpuc.org/construction-contracts/new-developments/stormwater-management)
- [Better Roofs Ordinance](https://sfplanning.org/project/better-roofs)
- [GoSolarSF](https://www.sfpuc.gov/accounts-services/sign-up-for-savings/gosolarsf)

# 9. Toronto, ON (Canada)

## Retention

**Regulatory Framework:** Toronto Green Standard (TGS) v4 (effective May 1, 2022); TRCA Stormwater Management Criteria.

**Triggers:**
- TGS v4 applies to Site Plan Control Applications with >2,000 m² gross floor area
- Residential: apartment buildings 4+ storeys; exempt for <5 units
- All ICI (industrial, commercial, institutional) developments must comply

**Tier 1 (Mandatory):**
- Retain minimum 50% of total average annual rainfall volume OR equivalent of 5mm from each rainfall event
- Through infiltration, evapotranspiration, water harvesting, and/or reuse

**Tier 2 (Voluntary):**
- Retain minimum 25mm from each rainfall event
- Compliant projects may be eligible for partial refund of development expenses

**Green Roof Performance:**
- Extensive systems: 5mm initial abstraction capacity
- Intensive systems: 7mm initial abstraction capacity
- Essential for meeting water balance on mid- and high-rise buildings

## Detention

**Peak Flow Control:**
- Post-development peaks must not exceed pre-development levels for 2, 5, 10, 25, 50, and 100-year storms

# 10. Ohio (Statewide)

## Retention

**Regulatory Framework:** Ohio EPA Construction General Permit (OHC000006, effective April 23, 2023; expires April 22, 2028); Ohio Rainwater and Land Development Manual.

**Post-Construction:** Permanent BMPs required with maintenance plan, inspection schedules, and recorded easements/agreements.

**Drawdown Times:**
- Dry Extended Detention Basins: 48-hour drawdown of WQv with <50% emptying in first 16 hours
- Wet Extended Detention/Wetland Basins: 24-hour minimum drawdown with <50% emptying in first 8 hours

**Critical Storm Method:**
- Peak rates for all 1–100 year frequency, 24-hour storms must not exceed pre-development rates

**Extended Detention:** Gradual release of 0.25–0.75 inch runoff per impervious acre over 24–48 hours.

# 12. Virginia (Statewide)

## Retention

**Regulatory Framework:** Virginia Stormwater Management Program (VSMP); 9VAC25-875; Virginia Runoff Reduction Method (VRRM) Version 4.1.

**Triggers:**
- Land-disturbing activities ≥10,000 sq ft
- Chesapeake Bay Preservation Area (CBPA): ≥2,500 sq ft

**Phosphorus Reduction Targets (effective July 1, 2025):**
- New development: must not exceed 0.26 lbs total phosphorus/acre/year
- Prior Developed Lands (≥1 acre, no net increase in impervious): minimum 20% phosphorus load reduction
- Prior Developed Lands (<1 acre): 10% reduction

**Virginia BMP Clearinghouse:** Approved BMPs with assigned phosphorus removal rates for use with VRRM spreadsheets. (As of July 1, 2024, website no longer updated; refer to DEQ website.)

## Detention

**Energy Balance Approach:**
- Applied when impervious area exceeds 7.5%
- QDeveloped ≤ I.F. × (QPre-Developed × RVPre-Developed) / RVDeveloped
- Improvement Factor: 0.8 for sites >1 acre; 0.9 for sites ≤1 acre

**Design Storms:** 1-year, 2-year, and 10-year, 24-hour storms.

**Channel Protection:** 2-year, 24-hour storm — post-development peak flow must not cause erosion.

**Flood Protection:** 10-year, 24-hour storm — post-development peak must not exceed pre-development peak.

## PV (Bare Roof / Vegetated Roof)

**Virginia Clean Economy Act (2020):**
- 100% clean electricity by 2050 for investor-owned utilities
- Residential system size increased to 20 kW; non-residential to 1 MW; agricultural to 500 kW

**Net Metering:**
- Dominion Energy: 1:1 monthly credit at full retail rate; max 25 kW; 6% aggregate capacity limit
- Appalachian Power: 1:1 retail rate for generation up to annual consumption; 5.66¢/kWh avoided cost for excess above annual usage
- NOTE: Dominion petitioned for NEM 2.0 changes (May 2025); SCC decision expected by May 2026

**SRECs:** 1 SREC per 1 MWh; sold to utilities for RPS compliance.

**Property Tax Exemptions:**
- Years 1–5: 80% of system value exempt
- Years 6–10: 70% exempt
- Year 11+: 60% exempt
- Systems ≤25 kW: automatic exemption

**Solar Rights:** HOAs cannot prohibit residential solar unless recorded declaration explicitly prohibits it. Restrictions cannot increase cost >5% or reduce production >10%.

**Community Solar:** Shared solar authorized; project max 5 MW; minimum 3 subscribers; total program cap 150 MW (+50 MW if 45 MW serves low-income).

**C-PACE:** 17 Virginia municipalities have implemented programs; up to 20+ year loan terms for commercial/multifamily/nonprofit. No residential R-PACE.

# 14. Montgomery County, MD

## Retention

**Regulatory Framework:** Maryland Stormwater Design Manual; Montgomery County Department of Environmental Protection.

**Triggers:**
- ≥5,000 sq ft of land disturbance
- Redevelopment: ≥5,000 sq ft where existing imperviousness >40%

**Environmental Site Design (ESD):**
- Primary strategy; implemented to Maximum Extent Practicable (MEP)
- Designed to replicate runoff hydrology of "woods in good condition" for the 1-year, 24-hour rainfall event
- Runoff Curve Number for woods in good condition = 55

**Water Quality Volume:** Varies by rainfall zone. Central/Eastern Maryland zone (Montgomery County): 1.5 inches precipitation depth for WQv sizing.

## Detention

**If ESD not fully met:**

**Channel Protection (Cpv):** 24-hour extended detention of the 1-year, 24-hour storm (12 hours in Use III/IV watersheds).

**Overbank Flood Protection (Q10):** Peak flow from 10-year storm controlled comparing proposed to existing conditions.

**Extreme Flood (Q100):** 100-year event safely conveyed; peak flow controlled comparing proposed to existing conditions.

## PV (Bare Roof / Vegetated Roof)

**IgCC Adoption:** Montgomery County adopted 2018 IgCC for commercial construction and additions ≥5,000 sq ft. Compliance via Zero Energy Performance Index (zEPI) score of 50 or lower.

**Maryland RPS:**
- 2025 solar carve-out: 7.0%; 2026: 8.0%
- Overall RPS: 38% (2025); 40.5% (2026); target 50% by 2030

**Net Metering (Pepco/BGE):**
- 3,000 MW statewide capacity limit
- System sizing: up to 110% of 12-month energy usage

**SRECs:** 1 SREC per 1 MWh; valid 15 years. Value tied to Solar Alternative Compliance Payment rates.

**Property Tax:** Maryland state credit = 100% of local property tax assessment increase for solar (effectively full exemption).

**Montgomery County Green Bank:**
- Montgomery Solar Loan: 30-year terms; 0% for low-income qualifying households; 4.99% for others
- Maryland Solar Access Program (MSAP): up to $7,500 grants ($750/kW) for income-eligible homeowners (≤150% AMI)

**Community Solar:** Maryland Community Solar Pilot Program now permanent. Minimum 40% of participants must be low- to middle-income. Available to homeowners, renters, businesses, nonprofits.

**C-PACE:** Available in Montgomery County for commercial, multifamily, and nonprofit properties.
